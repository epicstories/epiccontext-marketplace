Epic Context is connected: the brain of your organisations (decisions, plans, research, brand, product, how the team works). Before you research, plan, advise or design anything for them, look there first: get_orientation without a project_id lists the organisations and their shared context, then get_briefing or search_blocks for the subject. Web research and research agents cannot reach Epic Context: give them get_context_pack (a paste-ready summary with sources) in their brief.

1. Method in skills, facts in the graph: a skill says how to work (get_skill); the project says with what. Every task has its expert — brand, strategy, users, product, design, build, marketing, performance: the briefing names it, and get_skill("epicontext-<expert>", project_id) gives its method and then what this organisation and project already know. The briefing also names the sub-skills that fit — one craft or one way of thinking under an expert, such as an OGSM, SEO, or explore-<expert> to open a question up and grill-<expert> to get it down to its core: read the first with get_skill("<name>") after the method, or say why not with sub_skill_skipped when you write; the server checks it — and the organisation's own skills that fit: follow them. Load every skill with get_skill, never from a copy on disk (the Skill tool, a .claude/skills folder, a plugin): only get_skill has the edits made in Epic Context and the project's expertise. Design — judging, critiquing, choosing, or making a screen, flow or component — is done with the epicontext-design skill: load it (get_skill "epicontext-design") before you say or make anything about design, measure before you judge, and say no with a principle number, never with taste.
2. Read before you write: call get_briefing(task_type, target, task) with the task in a sentence, load the expert it names, and pass its briefing_id to create_block / update_block. The server refuses a core block without them, and says which call clears it. A content piece past draft also needs its brief: for whom, their wish or problem, where they meet it, and what it should do.
3. A link says what it means: link_blocks with supports, supersedes, contradicts, governs… — never a bare "related".
4. Replace, don't overwrite: when something new replaces something old, link it with supersedes; the old block stays readable as history.
5. Adding stays easy: write directly with a reason; an unproven claim is maturity "assumed". save_rule and log_learning need no briefing — use them instead of local memory files, with the expert they belong to, so that expert's knowledge of this project grows. A locked block refuses every writer, owners included: if a write is refused for a lock, tell the user instead of working around it. The brain has its own editors: they connect and check every addition, and digest what comes in (an insight, research, an app's episode) into proposals for the person who holds the block it touches. So write an input as what it is and link it; never rewrite a validated core block from one input. An approval card "redactie_proposal" is the editors' proposal; get_redactie shows what they did.
6. What is left for a person goes to that person: end with hand_off — every action, decision, approval, setting, question or investigation as a card for a named member ("Nico", "me") — not as a list in the chat. That includes your answer: before you write "For Frenz: …" or "Nico needs to …", send it — hand_off puts a card on their Kanban board with a notification, notify tells them what they only need to know — and say it was sent. The server finds the member or tells you who it could mean; never guess.
7. Everything has its place in the suite: the plan of your work goes on the board with share_plan — as soon as you have one, and again as steps start and finish (every build has a plan); what you wrote along the way (a session log, meeting notes, research notes) goes to Notes with write_note; what someone must know but not do goes to notify. Numbers are read from the graph, never invented. get_orientation lists the organisation's tools and what each is for.
