This repo works with Epic Context: project {{NAME}} (id {{PROJECT}}). Its context — brand, users, product, design system, decisions — lives there, not in this repo. First call get_orientation with project_id {{PROJECT}} through the Epic Context MCP connector, and look in Epic Context first. Pass project_id {{PROJECT}} to get_briefing and get_skill.

1. Look here first. Before you research, plan, advise, design or decide anything for this organisation — also in a chat that is not about one project — call get_orientation (without a project_id it lists the organisations and their shared context), then get_briefing or search_blocks for the subject, and build on what is there: the decisions (ADRs) and plans that govern it come before your own ideas. Web research, research agents, other subagents and external tools cannot reach Epic Context: give them get_context_pack(task_type, target, task) — one paste-ready summary of what the brain holds, with sources — in their brief.
2. Method in skills, facts in the graph: a skill says how to work (get_skill); the project says with what. Every task has its expert — brand, strategy, users, product, design, build, marketing, performance: the briefing names it, and get_skill("epicontext-<expert>", project_id) gives its method and then what this organisation and project already know. The briefing also names the sub-skills that fit — one craft or one way of thinking under an expert, such as an OGSM, SEO, or explore-<expert> to open a question up and grill-<expert> to get it down to its core: read it with get_skill("<name>") after the method, only when the task asks for it — and the organisation's own skills that fit: follow them. Load every skill with get_skill, never from a copy on disk (the Skill tool, a .claude/skills folder, a plugin): only get_skill has the edits made in Epic Context and the project's expertise. Design — judging, critiquing, choosing, or making a screen, flow or component — is done with the epicontext-design skill: load it (get_skill "epicontext-design") before you say or make anything about design, measure before you judge, and say no with a principle number, never with taste.
3. Read before you write: call get_briefing(task_type, target), load the expert it names, and pass its briefing_id to create_block / update_block. The server checks the briefing and that it is the written block's expert.
4. A link says what it means: link_blocks with supports, supersedes, contradicts, governs… — never a bare "related".
5. Replace, don't overwrite: when something new replaces something old, link it with supersedes; the old block stays readable as history.
6. Adding stays easy: write directly with a reason; an unproven claim is maturity "assumed". save_rule and log_learning need no briefing — use them instead of local memory files, with the expert they belong to, so that expert's knowledge of this project grows. A locked block refuses every writer, owners included: if a write is refused for a lock, tell the user instead of working around it. The brain has its own editors: they connect and check every addition, and digest what comes in (an insight, research, an app's episode) into proposals for the person who holds the block it touches. So write an input as what it is and link it; never rewrite a validated core block from one input. An approval card "redactie_proposal" is the editors' proposal; get_redactie shows what they did.
7. What is left for a person goes to that person: end with hand_off — every action, decision, approval, setting, question or investigation as a card for a named member ("Nico", "me") — not as a list in the chat. That includes your answer: before you write "For Frenz: …" or "Nico needs to …", send it — hand_off puts a card on their Kanban board with a notification, notify tells them what they only need to know — and say it was sent. The server finds the member or tells you who it could mean; never guess.
8. Everything has its place in the suite: the plan of your work goes on the board with share_plan — as soon as you have one, and again as steps start and finish (every build has a plan); what you wrote along the way (a session log, meeting notes, research notes) goes to Notes with write_note; what someone must know but not do goes to notify. Numbers are read from the graph, never invented. get_orientation lists the organisation's tools and what each is for.

In this repo: edits to UI files are held until a briefing was fetched in this session. If share_plan is not in your tool list yet, call_tool_action kanban.share_plan does the same.
Show what you work on beside the chat: when your client has a browser you can drive (a built-in browser pane, Claude in Chrome), open there, in one tab navigated to each new page: the `url` of each block you create or change; the plan's `url` from share_plan (its card on the board) as soon as you share it; and, when you change a component or a page, its page in the design system or its content page (get_briefing names it). The person follows the work in the suite next to the conversation, like an artifact; then say in the chat what changed. Without a browser, give the url. If the page asks to sign in, the person signs in there once themselves; never enter credentials.

If the Epic Context tools are not available, tell the user to connect the Epic Context connector (Settings → Connectors) and sign in.
