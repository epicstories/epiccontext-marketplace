# Analysis Flows — Brand Group

## Flow 1: Diagnose

### A. Diagnose from the graph

Common diagnoses:

**Voice drift across surfaces** — Different surfaces sound like different products.
- Pull content samples from at least 3 different surfaces (in-app, marketing, email, support)
- Apply tone-of-voice principles to each sample
- Where principles aren't met, document the gap
- Pattern: which surfaces drift most? Often the ones with no owner

**Positioning-product mismatch** — What we say vs. what the product actually does.
- Pull positioning + messaging
- Compare to actual product features and customer experience
- Where positioning claims something product doesn't deliver, that's a credibility risk

**Vision-strategy contradiction** — Vision and strategy say different things.
- Pull both. Read them together.
- Flag specific contradictions

**Messaging undifferentiated** — Sounds like every competitor.
- Pull messaging
- Compare to known competitor messaging in the category
- If you could swap brand names and the messaging still works for the competitor, it's not differentiated

### B. Diagnose from raw input

Inputs: brand workshops, customer feedback on brand, competitive analysis, sales conversation notes.

Process:
1. Extract candidate updates (positioning shifts, tone refinements, new messaging)
2. Test against existing brand entities — confirm, refine, or contradict
3. Surface contradictions explicitly

### Evidence-sufficiency gates

Before declaring a brand diagnosis:
- [ ] Multiple content samples or customer signals (not single complaint)
- [ ] Tested against alternative explanation
- [ ] Specific affected surfaces named
- [ ] Recent enough that current state reflected

## Flow 2: Summarize

Three audience formats:

**Internal:** Voice principles, current state, key drift signal, next move. Under 100 words.

**Team:** What's working, what's drifting, recommended adjustment. 200-350 words. Include 2-3 voice examples.

**Client:** Always include voice examples. Abstract principles without examples don't communicate brand. 1-2 pages.

## Flow 3: Recommend action

Default: recommend ONE action, explain why, name tradeoffs.

Brand recommendations have a long tail — every existing surface needs updating. Calibrate for that:

**Smallest — adjust voice principles or examples.** Document-only change; new content uses new principles, existing content gradually updates.

**Medium — refresh messaging.** Primary message stays; supporting messages refresh. Touches landing pages, key emails, sales enablement.

**Larger — reposition.** Customer language, value proposition, competitive frame all shift. Touches every surface eventually.

**Largest — revise vision.** Touches strategy, all brand expression, all communications. Usually a multi-quarter project.

Match recommendation size to evidence strength.

## Red Flags

**🚩 About to recommend "rebrand" from a few stakeholder complaints.**
Stop. Rebrands are expensive and disruptive. Most brand issues are voice drift or messaging refresh, not rebrand-level.

**🚩 Tone of voice document with no examples.**
Stop. Adjectives don't teach voice. Every principle needs do/don't examples.

**🚩 Positioning recommendation that doesn't address competitors.**
Stop. Positioning is competitive. Without naming alternatives, it's just description.

**🚩 Messaging that sounds great but isn't differentiated.**
Stop. Test: would this messaging work for a competitor? If yes, it's category messaging, not brand messaging.

**🚩 Vision shift recommended from one bad quarter.**
Stop. Vision is the foundation. Doesn't shift on quarterly variance. Usually the issue is execution or strategy, not vision.

**🚩 Brand assertion that contradicts product reality.**
Stop. "Fast" voice on a slow product. "Premium" positioning on a budget product. Either fix the product or revise the brand — don't claim what's untrue.

**🚩 About to write voice guidelines that try to be everything.**
Stop. "Warm AND authoritative AND playful AND serious" is no voice. Pick. Specificity is the discipline.

**🚩 Hedging language in voice principles.**
Stop. "We try to be approachable when appropriate" is unhelpful. Direct instruction: "We use first-person plural — 'we' not 'the company'."

## When to refuse

- Brand "audit" without sampling actual content
- Positioning recommendation from internal opinion alone (no customer or competitive evidence)
- Voice guidelines requested without time for example development
- Vision change requested as marketing exercise without strategic alignment

Honest output: "Not enough evidence at this confidence level. Here's what we'd need: [list]."

## Cross-group considerations

Brand-group signals (voice drift, positioning erosion, messaging not landing) sit at the intersection of how the project shows up and how it actually performs. Brand alone can't decide — Performance, Users, and Strategy must weigh in. See `epicontext-analysis/ANALYSIS.md` for the full method.

**Signals that typically originate here:**
- Voice drift across surfaces (different writers producing different voices)
- Positioning erosion (we sound like everyone else in the category)
- Messaging not landing (people don't repeat back the value prop accurately)
- Internal stakeholder confusion about "what we stand for"
- Brand audit reveals inconsistency between surfaces

**Pull from these groups before judging:**
- **Performance (engagement, conversion, AEO visibility):** does the brand voice/messaging actually move metrics, or is it self-pleasing?
- **Users (persona, JTBD):** does the voice match what the persona expects, or pull away?
- **Strategy + positioning bet:** is the "drift" actually the strategy evolving, or unintentional erosion?
- **Product (content, copy):** has the copy drifted, or has the voice itself changed?
- **Competitors:** does the positioning still differentiate, or has the category caught up?

**Trade-off types specific to this group:**
- Evolve voice vs hold the line (legitimate evolution vs erosion)
- Mass appeal vs distinctive (lower CAC short-term vs differentiation long-term)
- Single voice vs voice with register (consistency vs context-fit — see `decisions/voice-flexibility.md`)
- Brand assertion vs product reality ("we say X; product behaves Y" — fix one or the other)

**Default classification questions:**
- Is this drift, or evolution? (Drift = nobody decided; evolution = strategic choice)
- Does the voice reflect strategic positioning, or fight it?
- Does engagement data confirm the brand is working? (Or is brand a vanity layer?)
- Where would a customer recognize us by voice alone? (If "nowhere" — distinctiveness gap)
