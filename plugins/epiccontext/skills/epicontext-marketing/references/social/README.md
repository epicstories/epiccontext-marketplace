# Social strategy — organic social per workspace, from the brain

The method for an organic social strategy that comes **from the context**, not from a blank page: what social is for in this workspace, which platforms, which pillars, which series, how often, in which voice, how we learn, and what has to be built before any of it can run. It is the method of the Social tool's strategist and of anyone writing a social strategy by hand.

> The strategy is a proposal until a person accepts it. It lands as a plan (a `document`, category `plan`) beside the `marketing_strategy`, never over it. Accepting it is a person writing the channels, campaigns and content pieces it names.

## The rule above all

**Everything comes from the graph, nothing is invented.**
- Facts, prices, places and dates come from the product's data.
- The voice comes from `tone_of_voice`, the claims from `messaging_framework`, the audience from `persona` and `jtbd`.

Where the graph is empty, the strategy says so as a **gap**. It does not fill the gap with a guess dressed as a decision.

## The eight steps

### 1. The role of social here: one sentence

Social serves **one** goal of the OGSM, measured by a KPI the graph already has: awareness, consideration, conversion, retention or community.

- Name the journey stages social can touch. A journey step whose touchpoint is a social network is the clearest signal.
- Name the stages social cannot reach.
- Write what social is **not** for here (the anti-goal).

No goal in the graph is a gap, not a reason to pick "awareness".

### 2. Platforms: where the personas are, each with a role

Per persona, the platform they already use for the job. Choose **one to three** platforms; each gets one role:

| Role | What it is for | Typical fit (priors, see the evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | Reaching people who don't follow yet | Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts |
| **Depth** | Saves, shares, considered reading | Instagram carousels, LinkedIn document posts |
| **Trust** | The founder or the team as a person | A personal LinkedIn profile (far above a company page) |
| **Local or older reach** | Local audiences, deals, events | Facebook |
| **Search intent** | People looking for ideas or products | Pinterest, YouTube |
| **Direct line** | Followers who opted in | WhatsApp or Instagram channels |

"Social media" is not a platform, and "everywhere" is not a choice. A platform with no persona on it is cut.

### 3. Pillars: 3–5, each a persona × a moment × a message

A pillar is a recurring theme a person would follow for. Each pillar holds:
- **for whom:** a `persona`;
- **when:** a job from `jtbd` or a step of a `journey_map`, or a category entry point from the `marketing_strategy`;
- **what we say:** a claim from the `messaging_framework`;
- **the proof:** a `feature`, a `customer_insight` or the product's own data;
- **the share** of the posts.

The pillars together are MECE. A pillar without a persona is self-promotion; one without proof is a slogan.

### 4. Series and formats: repeatable, made from templates

A series beats a one-off: the same shape every week, with new content in it (*Deal of the week*, *One stop of a trip*, *Feature in 30 seconds*). Per series:
- **the format per platform:** reel, carousel, document post, story or still;
- **the hook:** the first frame or line, inside the platform's safe zone;
- **the template** that makes it. In the suite a template is made once and filled with data for every post. A series without a template is something to build (step 8);
- **the data the product must send** per post: its fields, and which of them are facts.

Adapt per platform; never cross-post the identical post. Never post near-duplicates (twenty cities with the same layout and only the name changed): that reads as spam, and the networks treat it so.

### 5. Cadence: consistency first, within what people can approve

1. **Consistency beats volume.** Skipped weeks cost more than a lower steady rate.
2. **The cadence is capped twice:**
   - by the content supply: what the product and the series really produce;
   - by the approval time the team really has: ask, and write the number in the strategy.
3. **Per platform, start at the evidence's priors** (Instagram 3–5 a week, TikTok 2–5, LinkedIn 1–3 from a person), then let the scoreboard move them.
4. **Posting times are hypotheses.** One test time a week keeps learning; a time counts after three scored posts.

### 6. Voice per platform, compliance always

- **The tone of voice is the brand's,** from `tone_of_voice`. A platform may make it tighter (LinkedIn: one question at the end, no CTA wall), never different.
- **The anti-claims** in the messaging framework hold everywhere.
- **Compliance is part of the strategy, not a check afterwards:**
  - the ad disclosure where the law asks for it (the Netherlands: on paid or affiliated posts);
  - an AI label on generated images or video (EU AI Act Art. 50);
  - licensed media only;
  - no fact, price or date that is not in the product's data.

### 7. Measure and learn, honestly at low volume

- **Every pillar** has a measure that ladders to the OGSM goal from step 1.
- **Scores are read at a fixed post age** (24 h, 72 h, 7 days) against the brand's own median, never across post ages.
- **One variable per experiment:** hook, format, template, posting time. Two arms; a winner only after enough posts per arm (about 6–10) and at least +20% on the measure.
- **No claims about "what works"** before about 30 posts per brand. Until then the scoreboard describes; it does not prescribe.
- **Each learning that holds** goes back to the graph as a `customer_insight`, or a rule for the Marketing expert.

### 8. What has to be built

The strategy is only real when what it needs exists. List every missing piece, each with **what**, **for which pillar or series**, **who builds it** and **why now**:

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| **A template** (the content tool) | A carousel for a weekly deals series; a reel for a trip-stop series |
| **Data from the product** | The fields a series needs, sent as data with every post |
| **Media** | Licensed photos, product shots, a voice-over |
| **An account or channel** | Connect the Instagram account; a channel block per platform |
| **Tracking** | UTM or referral on the landing pages, so a post's outcome can be counted |
| **Context** | An empty `tone_of_voice`, a persona without jobs, a journey without touchpoints, a goal without a KPI |

Order them by what unblocks the first pillar soonest. A context gap that blocks posting (an empty tone of voice) comes first.

## What the strategy delivers

1. **The strategy on one page:**
   - the role (step 1);
   - the platforms and their roles;
   - the pillars;
   - the series with formats and templates;
   - the cadence;
   - the voice rules;
   - the measures.
2. **Post ideas,** 5–10. Each names a pillar, a persona, a platform, a format, a hook, the template and why. Ideas go to the approval queue only through a person; the strategist proposes, people decide.
3. **What to build** (step 8), ordered, with owners.
4. **Gaps in the context,** each pointing at the block to fill.
5. **What would change it:** the evidence that would make this strategy wrong. Examples: a pillar under the median for six weeks; a platform where the persona turns out not to be.

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- A strategy without a goal from the OGSM, or with a goal the graph does not have.
- Every platform, the same post everywhere, or near-duplicate local posts.
- More posts a week than the content supply or the approval time allows.
- Chasing virality, trends or engagement bait the voice would never make.
- A number, a price, a place or a date that is not in the product's data.
- "Optimising" on a handful of posts, or comparing posts of different ages.
- Pillars that are departments or product modules instead of what a persona cares about.
- Writing over the `marketing_strategy` instead of proposing beside it.

## Evidence (priors, dated, not our data)

Treat these as starting points until the workspace's own scoreboard has about 30 posts.

| Claim | Source (date) | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Skipped weeks depress follower growth; consistency is the most reliable organic lever | Buffer, 4.8M channel-weeks (2025) | Correlational |
| Instagram: carousels lead on engagement per reach (6.9% vs Reels 3.3%); Reels reach more non-followers | Socialinsider, Q2 2026; Metricool 2026 | Overall Instagram reach is down in 2026 (Sotrender, August 2026) |
| Instagram: 3–5 posts a week is the sweet spot | Buffer (2025) | — |
| LinkedIn: document posts have the highest engagement (~6.6–7%, ~39% more reach); personal profiles far above company pages | AuthoredUp, 3M posts; Socialinsider (2026) | Carousel reach dipped in Q1 2026 |
| TikTok: 2–5 posts a week gives the biggest lift per post; 15–30 s clips have the highest engagement rate | Buffer, 11.4M posts; Socialinsider (2025–26) | Photo carousel vs video is contested: test it |
| Meta's safe zones: keep ~14% at the top and 35% at the bottom of a reel free of text | Meta (2025) | Changes with the app's interface |
