# Decision Tree: Focus or Expand?

The recurring strategic decision: double down on what's working, or add a new market/segment/capability?

## Start here

**Where is the project today?**

```
├─ Current focus is showing strong signal but not yet at scale
│   → A. Double down on focus
│
├─ Current focus has plateaued; growth has stalled
│   → B. Diagnose plateau before deciding
│
├─ Current focus is winning at scale; capacity for adjacent expansion
│   → C. Consider adjacent expansion
│
└─ Current focus is unclear or struggling
    → D. Don't expand; clarify focus first
```

## A. Double down on focus

**Best when:**
- Strong but early signal (good rebook rate, growing referrals, NPS holding)
- Capacity is the bottleneck more than market size
- Adjacent moves would split attention from what's working

**Approach:**
- Resist the temptation to expand prematurely
- Invest in scaling the working motion (more capacity, more efficient operations)
- Defer adjacent considerations to a future review

**Watch for:**
- Confusing "comfortable" with "working" — the current focus might just be familiar
- Imagined ceilings (it can't grow more) — usually wrong if signal is strong

## B. Diagnose plateau

**Best when:**
- Growth has stalled; not clear why
- Tempting to expand but the diagnosis hasn't been done

**Approach:**
- Run customer research to understand why growth stopped
- Check if it's saturation (real market ceiling) or self-imposed (operational, marketing, UX)
- DON'T expand into a new market while the current one is unexplained

**Plateau is usually:**
- Operational (can't deliver more without breaking quality) — fix operations
- Acquisition channel saturation (one channel maxed) — diversify channels in current market
- Real market saturation (rare in early-stage businesses) — then expansion makes sense

## C. Consider adjacent expansion

**Best when:**
- Current focus is genuinely winning at scale
- Real capacity exists for additional bets
- Adjacent expansion has clear strategic logic (same persona different job, same job different persona)

**Approach:**
- Treat as a major strategic decision — business case required
- Define the adjacency precisely (it's an extension, not a different business)
- Set explicit success/failure criteria for the adjacent bet
- Don't compromise the core focus

**Watch for:**
- "Adjacent" expansion that's actually a different business
- Founders' boredom — if you're tired of winning, that's not a reason to expand
- Expansion as ego ("we should be bigger")

## D. Don't expand; clarify focus first

**Best when:**
- Current focus is struggling or unclear
- Team is debating expansion partly because focus isn't working

**Approach:**
- Recognize that expansion under unclear focus produces TWO unclear bets
- Clarify the existing strategy first (revisit Strategy entity)
- Test the existing focus more rigorously before adding new bets

**The trap:** Expansion feels like progress. It often is the opposite of progress.

## The "what's working?" test

Before any expand decision:

1. Pull the OGSM. Are current Goals being hit?
2. Pull customer signals. Are existing customers staying and recommending?
3. Pull unit economics. Is the model profitable per unit?

If all three are yes: focus is working, choose A or C based on capacity.
If any are no: choose B or D. Diagnose first.

## Common failure modes this tree prevents

- **Expansion as escape from problems** — running from a struggling focus into a new struggle
- **Expansion before validation** — entering new market without proof current model works
- **Focus mistaken for stagnation** — assuming "we're doing the same thing" means we're not growing
- **Adjacent that's actually different** — calling a fundamentally different business "adjacent expansion"
- **Expansion driven by founder boredom** — internal motivation, external risk

## Quick guide

| Current state | Decision |
|---------------|----------|
| Strong early signal, capacity-limited | Double down |
| Growth plateau, unclear cause | Diagnose first |
| Winning at scale, capacity for more | Consider expansion |
| Struggling or unclear | Clarify focus first |
