# The project's design rules — step 2

Every project records, once, in its Design group — a `document` in the
Guidelines chapter, linked from the `design_system` overview:

- **Experience sentence** — the feeling and outcome the product is for, in one line (P1).
- **Slider stand** — one of five, with the reason (P2):

  | Stand | Name | Meaning |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | Tool | efficiency, neutral; no expression beyond the logo (expert tools, daily B2B) |
  | 2 | Useful with warmth | a tool, but with a recognisable tone and colour (a local index, a service) |
  | 3 | Balanced | expression and goal weigh the same |
  | 4 | Brand first | the experience may cost some ease: "not everything easy, but this is cool" |
  | 5 | Object | the product is the brand; campaign, launch, one-off |

  A stand may differ per flow (a stand-4 product's checkout is stand 2;
  a stand-1 tool's first run may be stand 3). The stand moves
  *expression*; interaction conventions are the same on every stand.
- **Style direction** — the active style card(s) (step 0).
- **Signature language** — shape, curve, motion character; where per flow it may become loud (P5).
- **Hierarchy rule for dense screens** — what stays uniform; frequency of use wins ties (P7).
- **Deliberate friction** — the points where friction is placed on purpose, for whose goal (definition, P6).
- **Optical corrections** — the recorded exceptions to the scale (P9).
- **Platform mix** — which conventions are native here.

If these are missing, propose them from the intake and have the user
confirm before landing them (`create_block` with a `reason` naming what
was read). Once they stand, every later design should be — in Robert's
words — *een eitje*: the hard choices were made once, at the system level.
