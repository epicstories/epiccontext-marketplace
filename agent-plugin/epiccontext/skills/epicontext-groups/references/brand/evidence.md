Cited data points from trusted sources for brand decisions.

### EVD-BRD-001 — Positioning is the most leveraged brand decision
- **Claim:** Dunford's framework: positioning has five components — competitive alternatives, differentiated capabilities, differentiated value, best-fit customers, and market category (with an optional sixth: trend). Positioning has higher leverage than tagline, brand identity, or feature messaging because it determines the competitive frame all other communication operates within.
- **Source:** April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (2019)
- **Citation:** Dunford, *Obviously Awesome: How to Nail Product Positioning So Customers Get It, Buy It, Love It*; [aprildunford.com](https://www.aprildunford.com/books)
- **Caveat:** Dunford's work is grounded in B2B technology positioning across 300+ engagements; framework applies broadly but the empirical base is B2B-tech-heavy.
- **Used in:** positioning, vision

### EVD-BRD-002 — Distinctive brand assets drive long-term recall
- **Claim:** Romaniuk's empirical work shows brands with strong distinctive assets (consistent visual, verbal, audio markers) achieve significantly higher customer recall in buying moments than brands relying on awareness alone.
- **Source:** Jenni Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (2018), Ehrenberg-Bass Institute
- **Citation:** Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets*
- **Used in:** tone-of-voice, messaging

### EVD-BRD-003 — Voice consistency aids comprehension and trust
- **Claim:** Inconsistent voice across product surfaces reduces user trust and slows comprehension. Consistent voice enables faster pattern recognition and lower cognitive load.
- **Source:** Erika Hall, *Conversational Design*; Sarah Richards, *Content Design*
- **Citation:** Hall, *Conversational Design*; Richards, *Content Design*
- **Used in:** tone-of-voice, content (Product group)

### EVD-BRD-004 — Specific positioning beats generic positioning in customer language
- **Claim:** Positioning that uses customer-specific language (the words they use to describe their problem) outperforms positioning in marketing language. Tested across landing page A/B tests, sales conversations.
- **Source:** Practitioner research; Joanna Wiebe (Copyhackers); Dunford
- **Caveat:** Specific lift numbers vary case to case; the directional finding is consistent.
- **Used in:** positioning, messaging

### EVD-BRD-005 — Brand assets work via mental availability
- **Claim:** Ehrenberg-Bass: brand growth comes primarily from being easy to think of in the buying moment (mental availability). Consistent brand assets across touchpoints increase the chance of being thought of.
- **Source:** Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow* (2010); Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets*
- **Citation:** Sharp, *How Brands Grow*
- **Used in:** positioning, messaging
- **Note:** Same source as EVD-USR-023 — cross-references the Users group evidence

### EVD-BRD-006 — Vision used for decisions outperforms vision used for marketing
- **Claim:** Cagan's work across product organizations: vision statements that get referenced during product decisions ("does this fit our vision?") drive better decisions than vision statements that exist only for the website.
- **Source:** Marty Cagan, *Empowered* (2020)
- **Citation:** Cagan, *Empowered: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products*
- **Used in:** vision

### EVD-BRD-007 — Tone documents need examples to be useful
- **Claim:** Voice and tone documents that contain only adjectives produce low writer adherence. Documents that contain do/don't pairs and worked examples produce significantly higher consistency. Documented across content design practitioner research.
- **Source:** Sarah Richards, Nicole Fenton, MailChimp's public Voice and Tone work
- **Citation:** Richards, *Content Design*; Fenton & Lee, *Nicely Said*
- **Used in:** tone-of-voice

### EVD-BRD-008 — Anthropic brand-voice plugin schema (operational)
- **Claim:** Anthropic's official brand-voice plugin (part of Knowledge Work Plugins) operationalizes voice in three skills: discover-brand (search across Notion/Drive/Confluence/Gong/Slack to find existing brand signals), generate-guidelines (synthesize discovered signals into a structured guideline document with "open questions" for unresolved positioning), and brand-voice-enforcement (apply guidelines to AI-generated content with explicit conflict-handling rules). Demonstrates that a usable voice system must be discoverable, generative, and enforceable — not just descriptive.
- **Source:** Anthropic — Knowledge Work Plugins (open source, Apache 2.0)
- **Citation:** [anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins — marketing/skills/brand-voice](https://github.com/anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins/tree/main/marketing/skills/brand-voice)
- **Caveat:** Anthropic's plugin is one operational implementation of a voice system; the underlying principles (do/don't pairs, examples, channel overrides) are not Anthropic-specific.
- **Used in:** tone-of-voice, voice-flexibility

### EVD-BRD-009 — Eugene Schwartz five awareness stages
- **Claim:** Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966) identifies five customer awareness stages that determine effective copy: Most Aware (knows product, price, ready to buy), Product Aware (knows product but unsure it solves their problem), Solution Aware (knows the problem can be solved but not by which solution), Problem Aware (feels the problem but hasn't named it), Unaware (doesn't realize they have the problem). Voice and message must match the stage; selling to "Unaware" with "buy now" copy fails.
- **Source:** Eugene Schwartz — *Breakthrough Advertising* (Boardroom Books, 1966)
- **Citation:** Schwartz, E.M. (1966). *Breakthrough Advertising*. Boardroom Books.
- **Caveat:** Foundational copywriting reference, but written for direct-response print advertising. Stages translate to digital but the recommended copy techniques are dated; the framework is what generalizes.
- **Used in:** voice-flexibility, messaging, content (Product group)

### EVD-BRD-010 — David Ogilvy advertising principles
- **Claim:** Ogilvy's principles, distilled across decades of advertising work: write to one specific reader (not "consumers"); the headline is 80% of the value (5x as many people read headlines as body copy); facts beat adjectives; long copy outperforms short copy when the buyer's investment is high; no advertisement should ever be conventional. Foundational to modern conversion copywriting and brand voice work.
- **Source:** David Ogilvy — Ogilvy & Mather; multiple books
- **Citation:** Ogilvy, D. (1963). *Confessions of an Advertising Man*; Ogilvy, D. (1983). *Ogilvy on Advertising*. Vintage Books.
- **Caveat:** Mid-20th-century print-and-broadcast era; not all tactics translate to digital channels. The principles about specificity, headlines, and facts do.
- **Used in:** tone-of-voice, voice-flexibility, messaging

### EVD-BRD-011 — Voice-of-customer copywriting (conversion school)
- **Claim:** Conversion copywriting practitioners (Joanna Wiebe / Copyhackers, the broader CRO field) demonstrate that the highest-converting copy comes from voice-of-customer research: interviews, reviews, support tickets, sales call transcripts. Mining customer language and using customers' exact words produces copy that resonates more than internally-generated phrasing. Established empirically through extensive A/B testing across SaaS, ecommerce, and information products.
- **Source:** Joanna Wiebe — Copyhackers; broader conversion copywriting practice
- **Citation:** [Copyhackers tutorials](https://copyhackers.com/); also referenced extensively in CXL Institute, Unbounce, Conversion Sciences materials.
- **Caveat:** Practitioner-driven; A/B test results are case-specific. The principle (customer language outperforms internal language) is well-supported; specific tactics vary.
- **Used in:** tone-of-voice, content (Product group), messaging
